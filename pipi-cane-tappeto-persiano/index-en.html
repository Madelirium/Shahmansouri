Has your dog peed on the rug?

When a dog pees on a Persian rug, the first thing to do is remove the liquid without making the rug worse: blot immediately, do not rub, do not use hot water and do not pour random products onto the fibres.

The first steps

  1. Blot, without rubbingUse white absorbent paper or a clean white cloth. Press gently on the wet area.
  2. Change the clothRepeat with dry paper or cloth while it still absorbs moisture. Do not scrub or pull the pile.
  3. Check underneath tooGently lift an edge and check whether the back or the floor is damp. If the rug is heavy or held down by furniture, do not force it.

Silk, antique rugs, kilims or delicate colours? After blotting, ask for advice before adding water or products.

Then, as my father always says, do not scold the poor dog too much: he often already has the face of someone who knows he has done something. Sometimes, at first, he does it to attract attention; then, if it happens again and again, it can become a habit. The rug, with a little luck, can be looked at, washed and repaired when possible… at worst, you throw it away and buy another one! A Persian rug, however, is not a wall-to-wall carpet. The pile, wool, colours, back, fringes and edges can react differently to moisture, heat and detergents. The problem is not only removing the stain: it is avoiding damage to the handmade rug.

If the rug is antique, fine, has silk areas, has already been restored or has delicate colours, greater caution is needed. In these cases, after the first steps to absorb the liquid, it is better not to insist with home remedies.

What to avoid

Urine needs to be absorbed, without pushing it deeper into the rug. Avoid these treatments.

  • Rubbing or brushing hard. This can spread the liquid and damage the pile.
  • Hot water and steam. Heat and added moisture can affect colours, fibres and old repairs.
  • Aggressive detergents or perfumes. Avoid bleach, ammonia, degreasers and products used only to cover the smell.

Masking the odour does not remove what has penetrated the pile or the structure. It may return later.

Which rugs need more caution?

Antique, fine, silk or previously restored rugs need greater care. Weak fringes, worn edges, stiff areas and uncertain colours are reasons to stop after the first blotting and ask for an opinion.

With kilims and other flatweaves, liquid can spread through the weave. The visible stain does not always show the full area affected.

Vinegar, baking soda and enzyme cleaners

These are not automatic remedies for a Persian rug. On fresh urine, the first task is to absorb the liquid.

Baking soda may be considered with caution for residual odours. Vinegar should never be assumed safe on every rug, even when diluted. With antique, fine, silk or uncertain-colour rugs, ask for advice before trying either.

Enzyme cleaners can act on organic substances, but not every product is suitable for wool, silk or delicate colours. Check compatibility with the material before considering their use.

When it is better to stop

Stop further cleaning attempts if you notice any of these signs.

  • The odour persists or returns after blotting.
  • A halo appears or the pile becomes stiff or changes colour.
  • The back remains wet.
  • Fringes, edges or previously repaired areas are weakened.

If moisture has reached the back, surface cleaning may not be enough. We first examine the front, back and affected area to understand whether washing or a more careful treatment is needed.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do immediately if my dog pees on a Persian rug?

Blot immediately with white absorbent paper or clean white cloths, without rubbing. Repeat until the cloth keeps absorbing moisture. Avoid hot water, steam cleaners and aggressive detergents.

Can I use baking soda on a Persian rug?

Not as the first step on fresh urine. The liquid must be absorbed first. Baking soda may be considered with caution only for residual odours and not on every rug.

Can I use white vinegar on a Persian rug?

Only with great caution, in a targeted and diluted way. On antique, fine or silk rugs, it is better to avoid home remedies before a proper check.

Is a steam cleaner safe?

No. On a Persian rug, a steam cleaner can be risky because it combines heat and moisture. It can trouble colours, fibres, weak areas or old restorations.

When should I ask for a check?

When the odour remains, a halo appears, the rug is antique or contains silk, or the back, fringes or edges look wet, stiff or weakened.
